# Presay User Guide

Presay pre-tests a page, ad, image or message on a panel of synthetic (AI-generated) personas that you define. You get a statistical read and a recommendation: accept the content as it is, or revise it, with the passages to change.

> Presay is in private preview. The respondents are simulated. Use results as a directional signal for comparing options and deciding what to test next, not as a substitute for research with real people. See [Limits of Synthetic Research](/limits.md).

## How a study works

1. **Describe the audience.** Pick a preset or build a mix of age, gender, geography, income, education and area. Choose 1 to 500 personas.
2. **Add the target.** A URL, image, pasted text, document, audio or video. Every persona sees exactly the same thing.
3. **Presay runs the simulation.** Each persona reacts in character and scores **clarity**, **appeal**, **trust** and **relevance** from 1 to 5.
4. **Read the report.** You get the decision, the scores with 95% bootstrap intervals, a segment breakdown, a narrative that cites its evidence, and every individual reaction. Share it as a read-only link or export it.
5. **Act on it.** Turn the recommendations into an improved draft and score it against the original.

## Key ideas in one minute

- A **persona** is a simulated respondent. A **panel** is the personas generated for one study.
- A **study** is one run of one target (or two, for an A/B test) against one panel. It counts once against your [allowance](/account/limits.md).
- The **directional signal** (0 to 100) summarises how favourably the panel reacted. Compare it between versions. It is not a forecast.
- Compare options and read the **intervals**, not the single number. See [Metrics](/reports/metrics.md).

More terms are in the [Glossary](/glossary.md).
