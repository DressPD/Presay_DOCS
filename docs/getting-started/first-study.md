# Your First Study

This walkthrough tests a headline on a general-population panel and shows what to look at in the result. It takes about ten minutes, including reading the report. In a hurry? The [Quick Start](/getting-started/quick-start.md) is the short version.

**Before you start:** [create an account](/getting-started/account.md) and have a piece of text ready. If you do not have one, use this:

> Stop losing leads: reply to every enquiry in under a minute, automatically.

## 1. Start a new study

Open **Studies** and choose **New study**. Pick what you want to test:

- **A/B test** to compare two versions
- **Concept test** for an idea, feature or product concept
- **Messaging test** for one headline, description or piece of copy

Choose **Messaging test** for this walkthrough. See [Study Types](/studies/types.md) for the differences.

## 2. Add your content

Paste your text into **Target text**. You can also use a URL, an image, a document, or audio and video. See [Choosing a Target](/studies/targets.md).

A name is optional, but it makes the study easy to find later. Something like "Lead response headline v1" works.

## 3. Choose an audience

Under **Demographics**, pick a **Preset** or choose **Custom** to build your own age, gender, geography, income, education and area mix. For a first study, a preset for the country you sell in is fine. See [Audience & Sample Size](/studies/audience.md).

## 4. Choose how many personas

Set **Sample size (n)** between 1 and 500. The form shows the worst-case margin of error and the confidence tier for your choice before you run.

For a first pass, use **20 to 30**. It is quick and tells you which way the content leans. Larger samples are tighter but take longer. See [Plan a Good Study](/studies/plan.md) for how to choose.

## 5. Run

Choose **Run study**. The form shows how many studies you have left in your [allowance](/account/limits.md) and an estimate of how long the run will take. A study of 20 to 30 personas usually takes about a minute. You can leave the page: the study keeps running.

## 6. Read the report

When the study completes, open it from **Studies**. Read in this order:

1. **The decision.** **Accept** or **Revise**. If it says Revise, the recommendations are ranked High, Medium and Low.
2. **The four scores.** Clarity, appeal, trust and relevance, each with an interval. Look for the lowest one.
3. **The narrative.** Pick one statement and follow its citation to the persona reactions behind it.
4. **Methodology & sample.** Confirm how many personas returned a usable response and what was analysed.

[Reading a Report](/reports/reading.md) covers each section, and [From Report to Decision](/reports/playbook.md) shows how to act on what you find.

## 7. Improve and retest

If the decision was **Revise**, open **Draft an improved version**. For pasted text, **Apply and retest** rewrites your text and scores the rewrite against the original on the same panel, as one study. See [Improve & Retest](/drafts/retest.md).

## Common first-run mistakes

- **Testing too much at once.** Paste the headline, not the whole page.
- **Reading one number as the answer.** Look at the intervals and the narrative.
- **Re-running the same content to "get a better score".** Run-to-run variation is normal. Change the content, then retest.

> Results are simulated and directional. Use them to compare options and decide what to test next.
