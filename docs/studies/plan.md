# Plan a Good Study

A few minutes of planning decides whether a study gives you something you can act on. Use this page before you press **Run study**.

## 1. Start with a question

Write the question in one sentence before you set anything up. The study type follows from it.

| Your question | Study type |
| --- | --- |
| "Does this headline, email subject or paragraph land?" | [Messaging test](/studies/types.md) |
| "Is this idea, feature or product concept appealing?" | [Concept test](/studies/types.md) |
| "Which of these two versions is better?" | [A/B test](/studies/types.md) |
| "Does this page make sense to a first-time visitor?" | Messaging test with the page URL |
| "Did my rewrite actually improve it?" | [Improve & Retest](/drafts/retest.md) |

If you cannot phrase the question, you are not ready to run the study. Presay returns a read on how the content lands. It cannot tell you what you should have asked.

## 2. Test one thing at a time

- **Change one thing between A and B.** If two versions differ in headline, image and price, you will not know which change moved the result.
- **Change substance, not just phrasing.** The panel does not react to wording the way real people do, and two versions that are mostly the same text mostly differ in wording. See [Limits of Synthetic Research](/limits.md).
- **Test the shape you will ship.** A headline test needs the headline, not the whole page. See [Choosing a Target](/studies/targets.md).

## 3. Describe the people you reach

- Start from a **preset** for a general audience in the right country.
- Use a **custom** mix when you know who actually sees your content, for example the age and income split of your real customers. Age, gender and geography are required.
- If you leave income, education or area empty, Presay fills them with general-population defaults for the region and lists them in the report's methodology section.
- Set **relationship to the brand** and **brand awareness** only when they matter, for example when you are testing a message for people who have never heard of you.

See [Audience & Sample Size](/studies/audience.md). To reuse a mix, save it as a preset or keep it in the [Audience Library](/audiences/library.md).

## 4. Pick a sample size for the decision

| You want to | Sample size | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get a first read and see which way it leans | 20 to 29 | Directional |
| Trust the overall direction | 30 to 99 | Indicative |
| Quote a result, or compare segments | 100 or more | Robust |

Segments with fewer than 5 personas are hidden, so if you care about one group, make sure the panel includes enough of them. In an A/B test the sample size is the number **per variant**.

## 5. Spend your allowance deliberately

Every study counts toward a rolling allowance of 30 a week and 10 a day. A good rhythm:

1. **Explore small.** Run 20 to 30 personas to see whether the content is in the right area.
2. **Fix what the report points to.** Use [Improve & Retest](/drafts/retest.md) instead of re-running the same content hoping for a different number.
3. **Confirm bigger.** Run a larger sample on the version you plan to ship.

An A/B test, **Apply and retest** and **Score against original** each count as one study. See [Study Allowance](/account/limits.md).

## Checklist

- [ ] I wrote down the question this study answers.
- [ ] The target is the thing I will actually ship.
- [ ] If I am comparing versions, they differ in one meaningful way.
- [ ] The audience matches the people who will see it.
- [ ] The sample size fits the decision (and any segment I care about has enough personas).
- [ ] I know what result would make me change my mind.
