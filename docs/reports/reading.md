# Reading a Report

A report is laid out from the answer down to the evidence. Read it top to bottom the first time.

## 1. Decision and directional signal

A report on a single target leads with an [accept-or-revise call](/reports/decision.md). Beside it is the **directional signal**, a 0 to 100 summary of how favourably the simulated panel reacted across all four metrics.

Use the signal to compare variants. It is not an absolute score or a forecast.

## 2. Per-metric scores

Each persona rates four things on a 1 to 5 scale, where higher is always better. See [Metrics](/reports/metrics.md) for what each means.

For each metric the report shows:

- the **mean** (the bar)
- the **95% bootstrap interval** (the lighter band)
- the share who rated 4 or 5
- an interpretation: **Strong**, **Moderate** or **Needs improvement**
- how it compares with your own studies: **Above your average**, **Typical for you** or **Below your average**, based on your last completed studies

The bootstrap interval resamples the simulated personas to show how far the mean moves across them. It is **not** a margin of error for a real population.

A metric with fewer than 5 responses is suppressed.

## 3. Score spread

The panel's scores usually spread out less than real respondents' do. The spread panel shows the share of ratings at 1 to 2 and at 4 to 5. Read it as the shape of the panel's answers, not a forecast of how real people would vote.

## 4. Narrative synthesis

A written summary of what the panel said. Every statement lists the **cited reactions** the model picked as support, so you can trace a claim to specific personas.

A citation is the model's own judgment, not a statistical test. Check it against the full list of reactions.

## 5. Recommendations

Prioritised changes (**High**, **Medium**, **Low**), each with the evidence behind it, the expected effect, the passage it concerns and watch-outs. They are drafted from the results, then checked against them by a second model pass.

## 6. Segments and reactions

[Segments & Reactions](/reports/segments.md) covers the breakdown by age, gender and area and the grid of individual persona reactions.

## 7. Methodology and sample

Open **Methodology & sample** to see:

- the exact content each persona received (**Analysed content**)
- the model used and the interval method
- whether the sample matched the requested weights, and where it deviated
- which dimensions used general-population defaults because you left them unspecified
- how many personas returned a usable response

## Notes under the numbers

The report adds validity notes where they apply, for example that the panel does not react to wording like real people do, that one model builds the panel and writes the report, or that the personas saw page text only. They are listed in [Limits of Synthetic Research](/limits.md).
