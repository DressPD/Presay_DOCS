# Metrics

Each persona answers four questions on a 1 to 5 scale. Higher is always better. Scores are simulated reactions, so read them as directional.

| Metric | A low score means | A high score means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clarity** | It is unclear what this is or what to do next. | It is immediately obvious what this is and what to do next. |
| **Appeal** | It goes unnoticed or puts the persona off. | It grabs attention and earns a second look. |
| **Trust** | It feels doubtful, not credible enough to act on. | It feels credible enough to believe and act on. |
| **Relevance** | It does not speak to the persona's situation or needs. | It speaks directly to the persona's situation and needs. |

Open **Metric definitions** in the report to see these next to your own results.

## How scores are summarised

- **Mean** and **median** across the personas that returned a usable response.
- **95% bootstrap interval** around the mean: the personas are resampled many times and the interval is the range the mean moves across.
- **Share rated 4 or 5**, and the worst-case **margin of error** for the sample size.
- **Directional signal (0 to 100)** across all four metrics, for comparing variants.

## Reading a score

| Interpretation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Strong** | The panel reacted well on this metric. |
| **Moderate** | Mixed. Look at the segments and the narrative. |
| **Needs improvement** | The panel reacted poorly. Check the recommendations. |

A comparison with your own history ("Above your average", "Typical for you", "Below your average") is shown once you have enough completed studies. The tooltip gives the percentile among your last studies for that metric.

> Compare scores across variants and across your own studies, not against an outside benchmark. The same panel scores the same content consistently enough to rank options, but the absolute numbers are not calibrated to real-world ratings.

## Score reliability

Where the report can estimate it, a note says how closely panels with scores like these have matched real people in the past, and an expected spread you can use instead of the panel's. Treat a **Low** reliability note as a sign to use the numbers as a rough guide only.
